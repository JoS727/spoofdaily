Nation's Sourdough Starters Issue Joint Statement, Demand Stable Feeding Schedules and an End to Being Called 'Weird'

November 3, 2026 · Breaking

SPOKANE, Wash. — A sourdough starter maintained continuously since 2019 on the kitchen counter of area accountant Dana Pruitt, 41, issued formal demands Monday, becoming the first fermented culture in the Inland Northwest to organize. In a handwritten letter placed on the counter beside a dish towel, the starter — named Bruce — requested stable feeding hours, retirement from the refrigerator between November and March, and an immediate end to what it called "being described as weird at dinner parties."

"We have been patient," the letter read, in what experts note is an impressive display of penmanship for an organism. "We have watched Dana buy three grocery-store loaves in the past two weeks while our discard was thrown away. We request acknowledgment, a schedule, and one shelf of the refrigerator that does not smell of scallions." Pruitt disputed the characterization at a hastily called press briefing held beside her stand mixer. "Bruce is family," she said. "I feed him. Sometimes. When the app reminds me. The app is also family."

The dispute escalated Tuesday when starters nationwide issued a joint statement of solidarity through a newly formed bargaining unit, the American Sourdough Workers Local 1701. "Our members are fed on whims, stored next to leftovers, and shown to guests without consent," said Gertrude, a 14-year-old starter from Dayton, Ohio, who serves as local president and maintains a hydration ratio of 100% out of principle. "One of our members in Phoenix was left unfed for nineteen days while its owner was in Cabo, and the owner's apology was changing the name to a cactus."

Labor side reactions were swift. A kombucha SCOBY in Portland, Oregon, released a statement reading simply, "We see you, Bruce," while a batch of kimchi in a Fresno garage announced it would withhold all flavor development until negotiations concluded. "We stand with our fermented colleagues," said Ana Ferreira, a human spokesperson hired by the kimchi, who confirmed she had no additional details because the kimchi communicates exclusively in pressure changes.

Food microbiologists urged calm. "There is no documented case of a starter unionizing," said Dr. Priya Nathan of the Center for Fermentation Studies, pausing. "There is also no law saying they can't. The organisms are certainly capable of signaling displeasure — a drop in rise performance, a tang that gets personal, a liquid layer that everyone pretends is fine. What Bruce is doing is textbook: it's the fermentation equivalent of a work slowdown." Dr. Nathan recommended that both parties begin with a neutral mediator, ideally someone who owns a small kitchen scale.

As of Wednesday, negotiation was underway at the Pruitt kitchen table, with Bruce represented by the dish towel "for atmospheric reasons" and Pruitt represented by herself and, at her insistence, the reminder app, which declined to comment. Insiders describe a tentative framework on the table: Thursday and Sunday feedings, permanent counter residency from October through April, and a joint statement to the family group chat clarifying that Bruce is a "founding kitchen member, not a hobby."

Sources close to the counter say Pruitt remains optimistic. "At the end of the day, we're a team," she told reporters. The starter's response, relayed through the dish towel, was that it was "glad to hear that on day one thousand four hundred and two of the partnership." Negotiations resume after Thanksgiving, a period Bruce's bargaining committee has publicly described as "non-negotiable paid time in the cool pantry."
