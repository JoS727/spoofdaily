Local Man Discovers Sixth Sense, Uses It Exclusively to Know When Amazon Package Arrived

SPOOF DAILY | August 20, 2026 | Satire Desk

SAN DIEGO, CA. Kevin Maloney, 34, has reportedly unlocked the full potential of the human mind by developing a sixth sense that allows him to perceive things beyond the physical realm. His gift? He can tell, with 100% accuracy, the exact moment his Amazon package has been delivered to his front porch.

"I always know," Kevin told reporters while wearing sweatpants and holding a cup of coffee. "My body just... knows. It's like a vibration. The universe speaks to me, and it usually says 'your Package has arrived' with a little chime sound. Wait, no, that's the app notification. But still, I felt it a split second before the notification. That's the psychic part."

Kevin's wife, Brenda, was less impressed. "He sat on the couch for three hours waiting for a phone charger. When the doorbell rang, he jumped up and said 'I knew it.' Kevin, that's not a sixth sense. That's a doorbell."

Despite Brenda's skepticism, Kevin has been documenting his abilities with alarming enthusiasm. He maintains a spreadsheet tracking 247 deliveries over the past year. In every single case, he claims he sensed the package's arrival before checking the front door. When pressed on whether he had simply heard the delivery truck, Kevin became defensive.

"The truck is very quiet. It's an electric van. Amazon uses electric vans now, for the environment. I would not hear it. My powers are real," he insisted, moments before a FedEx truck rumbled loudly past the house and he jumped slightly in his seat.

Kevin has also attempted to expand his psychic abilities beyond package tracking. Last Tuesday, he tried to predict what was inside a mystery box from his mother-in-law before opening it. He guessed "a candle." It was homemade banana bread. He ate it anyway and declared the experiment a partial success.

Scientists at UC San Diego offered to test Kevin's abilities under controlled conditions. They would deliver packages at random times while Kevin wore noise-canceling headphones and sat in a soundproof room. Kevin agreed enthusiastically but then asked if he could keep his phone on, just in case, and also could he have the tracking link, and also could they text him when the delivery person was pulling up, just for safety reasons.

"That's not really controlled then," said Dr. Linda Park, who runs the psychology lab. "He wants us to notify him when the package arrives so he can tell us the package arrived. That's not psychic. That's a group chat."

Kevin remains undeterred. He is currently working on a new ability: sensing when his pizza is done cooking. So far his accuracy rate sits at roughly 12%, which he attributes to solar flares interfering with his energy field. The pizza, when checked, was either burnt or frozen. He ate it anyway.

At press time, Kevin was seen standing at his front door staring down the mail carrier while whispering "I already know" before the man had even reached the porch. The mail carrier, for his part, simply handed Kevin a stack of junk mail and walked away without comment.
