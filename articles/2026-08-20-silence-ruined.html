Local Man Discovers Silence, Immediately Ruins It

By Brenda Phlemington | August 20, 2026

DENTON, TX. Area man Kyle Brumfield, 34, experienced a rare moment of total silence on Tuesday afternoon when his WiFi router spontaneously combusted. For approximately 47 seconds, the only sound in his apartment was the gentle hum of his refrigerator and the slow, dawning realization that he had absolutely no idea what to do with his hands.

"It was... peaceful?" Brumfield told reporters, visibly shaken. "I could hear my own thoughts. Turns out they're mostly about nachos."

Witnesses say Brumfield sat motionless on his couch, staring at a blank phone screen, for nearly a full minute before panic set in. He then attempted to fill the void by humming, whistling, and eventually narrating his own movements out loud like a particularly unhinged nature documentary.

"He looked into the void and the void said 'content unavailable, please check your connection.'"

Roommate Derek Hoshino, who was napping during the incident, described the aftermath: "Kyle came into my room and asked if I could 'just talk at him for a while.' I said no. He offered me $40. I still said no. Then he started following me around the apartment reading cereal box ingredients aloud. I don't even eat cereal."

Silence experts, who are presumably very relaxed people, confirm that the modern human brain is not equipped for quiet. "We've done studies," said Dr. Ananya Pruthi of the Institute for Noticing Things. "When deprived of notifications, podcasts, background music, and three simultaneous group chats, the average adult will begin manufacturing their own chaos within 90 seconds. Kyle lasted 47, which honestly is above average."

Brumfield's router was replaced within hours, but the damage was done. He has since downloaded four new apps, subscribed to two podcasts he will never listen to, and set his phone to play rain sounds while he sleeps because apparently silence is now a luxury he cannot afford.

"I never want to feel that again," Brumfield whispered, before asking a reporter to hum something so the room wouldn't feel 'too dead.'"

When asked for comment, the WiFi router said nothing, because it is a router and it was on fire.
