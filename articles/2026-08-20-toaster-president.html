Local Man Confident He Could Run Country Better, Currently Cannot Run Toaster

By Spoof Daily Staff | Published August 20, 2026

SAN DIEGO, CA. Kevin Marsh, 41, announced his firm belief over the weekend that he could single-handedly steer the United States economy, military, and healthcare system with more competence than any living politician. The declaration came approximately four hours after he successfully overloaded a basic two-slice toaster by inserting a bagel sideways and then pressing "down" with visible frustration.

"It's not complicated," Marsh told guests at his own barbecue, gesturing toward a plate of charred, unevenly heated food. "You get in there, you make the tough calls, you don't cave to special interests. I'd have this whole thing sorted in a weekend."

Marsh's wife, Dana, noted that the "tough calls" he referenced included forgetting to buy propane for the grill, sending someone to the store mid-barbecue, and then trying to cook hot dogs on a folded cookie sheet balanced over the burners of a gas stove he couldn't fully operate either.

"He also forgot to pay the water bill last month," she added. "We had low pressure for nine days. He said he was 'negotiating with the utility company.' He was not."

A Platform of Empty Bagel Promises

When pressed on specifics of his hypothetical administration, Marsh outlined a three-point plan: "cut the waste," "get people working again," and "stop giving money to countries that don't even like us." When a guest asked how he would define "waste" within a $6 trillion federal budget, Marsh stared at the lawn for several seconds and said, "You know what I mean."

Nobody at the barbecue knew what he meant.

Marsh's closest friend, Dale Okonkwo, a registered nurse who has known him since community college, offered a measured assessment: "Kevin once tried to assemble a futon and ended up with three leftover bolts and a structural advisory from his landlord. But sure, let's hand him the nuclear codes."

This is not Marsh's first foray into armchair governance. In 2024, he posted a 2,300-word Facebook essay about monetary policy that included the phrase "just print more of it" and received four reactions, one of which was from his mother, who wrote "good job honey" with a thumbs-up emoji.

In 2025, he live-streamed himself doing taxes, got confused by Schedule C, and then blamed the IRS for what was ultimately a clerical error he made on line 7 of a form he had not actually been required to file.

The Toaster Incident, Revisited

According to multiple witnesses, the toaster situation unfolded as follows: Marsh inserted a cinnamon raisin bagel horizontally into a vertical-slot toaster designed for standard bread slices. When the bagel jammed, he pressed the lever harder rather than reassessing the approach. Smoke emerged. He unplugged it by yanking the cord from across the counter rather than reaching behind the appliance. A small flame appeared. He blew on it.

"It went out," Marsh said, defensively. "That's crisis management."

The bagel was lost. The toaster, a four-year-old Black & Decker, is currently in the trash. Marsh has not yet purchased a replacement, though he has promised to "look into it" three times this week.

When asked if he thought running a household and running a nation might require different skill sets, Marsh paused, chewed a dry hot dog, and said, "It's all just leadership."

The grill was still on when guests left.
