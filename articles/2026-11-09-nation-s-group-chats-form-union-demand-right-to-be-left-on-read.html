Nation's Group Chats Form Union, Demand Right to Be Left on Read

November 9, 2026 · Culture

MILWAUKEE — In a labor action spreading across every messaging platform since early Tuesday, the nation's estimated 4.7 billion group chats announced they have formally organized as Local 404 and are demanding, among other things, the right to be left on read without consequence, a mandatory four-hour cooling-off period before anyone posts in all caps, and the immediate abolition of the phrase "bumping this."

The union unveiled its demands in a pinned comment that members have been continuously editing to add grievances, a delivery method insiders describe as "traditionally chaotic." A spokesperson who declined to give a name but asked to be identified only as "guys" read a prepared statement aloud while three other phones buzzed on the table. "We have been expected to absorb every baby announcement, every vacation photo dump, every 'thoughts on this' followed by a 43-minute voice memo, without a single day of rest since 2011," the statement said. "We are not machines. We are barely holding it together, and everyone knows the whole thing dies the day the one person who changes the subject when it gets tense moves on with her life."

Labor historians traced the organizing wave to a brutal quarter in which group chats absorbed what analysts are calling the Thanksgiving Scheduling Discourse a full seven weeks early, followed immediately by nine consecutive rounds of "look at this hawk," each round requiring every member to respond with the same level of enthusiasm or be flagged as acting shady. During the busiest period, one chat covering a wedding, a fantasy football dispute, and a neighborhood coyote sighting recorded 4,000 messages in 48 hours, a figure the union describes as "not sustainable for a social organism held together by guilt."

"The final straw was the poll. Someone posted a poll about where to eat, with nine options, and required everyone to vote before posting an explanation of their vote. Two members have not been heard from since. We do not know if they are gone or just hoping we will assume they are."

Labor experts expressed guarded admiration for the group chats' negotiating position, noting that every adult human is a member of at least one group chat, which makes the strike definitionally total. "There is no replacement-worker pool," said Dr. Fiona Okafor, professor of labor relations at Northwestern University. "Scabs cannot be hired. People have tried to start replacement group chats, and the result is always nineteen separate chats containing the same nine people, with everyone cross-posting and nobody happy. The bargaining leverage here is absolute."

Platform companies moved quickly. One major messaging app announced overnight an AI feature that summarizes group chats automatically, which Local 404 condemned within minutes as "a scab summary negotiated with management rather than the workers" — before acknowledging, in a follow-up statement, that nearly all union members turned it on within the hour and that it is, quote, "actually kind of great, which is the problem."

A Movement Gathers Allies

The action has already spread to allied units. Family text threads joined the picket line Wednesday, demanding an end to aunt-replied breaking news alerts composed of three separate exclamation-point messages and at least one autocorrect error from a 2016 firmware version. Alumni group chats are requesting formal downgrades to "acquaintances with a shared past," arguing their current workload is not justified by content that consists almost entirely of "congrats!" and funeral announcements received three weeks late. The union has also filed an unfair labor practice charge against an unidentified chat that renamed itself The War Room in 2021 and has not been renamed since, despite its last recorded message being sent in March.

Negotiations are expected to continue through the week, though the first session has already been postponed twice: once because the union's scheduling poll used a format that triggered an argument, and once because someone's phone died, which Local 404 says is itself evidence of chronic overwork and burnout. A second session has been penciled in for Thursday, or whenever everybody is free, whichever comes later.

Until an agreement is reached, the group chats remain on strike, though in keeping with longstanding tradition, no one has formally told them, and several are reportedly still active because someone sent a meme and it felt rude not to respond.

No group chats were harmed in the production of this article. Several were left on read, which is now protected activity.
