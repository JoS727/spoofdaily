Local Man Discovers Meaning of Life, Forgets to Write It Down

COLUMBUS, OH · August 20, 2026 · SpoofDaily Staff

COLUMBUS, OH · Area man Derek Hollings, 34, reportedly achieved the single greatest philosophical breakthrough in human history at approximately 2:47 AM last Tuesday while standing in his kitchen eating dry cereal out of the box. Unfortunately, by the time he reached a pen, he had already forgotten it.

"It was beautiful," Hollings told reporters, staring at a spot on the wall approximately six inches to the left of everyone he was speaking to. "It all made sense. Everything. Why we're here. Why the socks always disappear. Why the cat stares at corners. It was so clear. Like a window opened in my brain and a cool breeze just... rolled through."

According to Hollings, the revelation occurred during a perfectly ordinary moment. He had gotten up for a glass of water, noticed the refrigerator light was still on, and suddenly understood the fundamental interconnectedness of all matter, consciousness, and the reason his landlord keeps ignoring his texts about the dishwasher.

"I tried to hold onto it. I really did. But then the cat knocked something off the counter and by the time I turned back around, it was just... vibes. Nothing but vibes."

Witnesses confirm that Hollings spent the next several minutes pacing the kitchen, opening and closing his mouth, and repeatedly snapping his fingers while whispering "it's on the tip of my tongue" to no one in particular. His roommate, Kyle Denton, 31, was asleep during the event and learned about it the following morning via a series of increasingly frantic text messages.

"He sent me 47 texts between 2:50 and 3:15 AM," said Denton, rubbing his temples. "The first one said 'I figured it all out.' The last one said 'nvm I think it was about cheese.' I don't know what I'm supposed to do with that."

Philosophers from several major universities have expressed mild interest in Hollings' account, though most agree the incident is consistent with a well-documented phenomenon known as "shower thoughts," which produces approximately 4.2 billion profound realizations per day globally, of which exactly zero have ever been successfully transcribed.

"The human brain is remarkable at generating cosmic truth under specific conditions," said Dr. Anita Reyes, a cognitive scientist at Ohio State who agreed to comment only because she found the story funny. "Unfortunately, those conditions are almost always: slightly hungry, mildly dehydrated, standing in dim lighting, and not near any recording device. It is essentially the same circuit that makes you think you could definitely win a fight against a goose. It feels real. It is not."

Hollings has since purchased a pocket notebook and keeps it on his nightstand. He has used it exclusively to write down grocery lists and one drawing of what he describes as "a cool mushroom." He remains convinced the meaning of life will return to him eventually, possibly during his next trip to the hardware store.

Maybe try writing it down next time. Get the notebook.

Because the universe doesn't care if you remember.

As of press time, Hollings was standing in his kitchen again, staring at the refrigerator light, and insisting it was "coming back to him."
