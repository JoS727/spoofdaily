Local Man Discovers He Is the Main Character, Immediately Forgets Side Quest

By The Department of Unresolved Plot Threads · August 20, 2026

SAN DIEGO, CA · Local man Kevin Brundle, 34, achieved total cosmic awareness Tuesday afternoon when a bird landed on his windowsill and made direct eye contact for approximately four seconds. In that brief and holy interval, Kevin reported seeing the entire structure of reality unfold before him like a deleted scenes menu, confirming what he had long suspected: he is the main character of the universe and everyone else is an NPC with diminishing polygon counts.

"It was profound," Kevin told reporters while standing in the middle of a crosswalk because the walk signal had not yet been programmed for his narrative arc. "I always knew I was the protagonist. Traffic doesn't stop for background characters. My coffee order being wrong every single time at the place on Fourth Street? That was dramatic tension. My ex texting me at 2 AM last Thursday? That was a subplot trigger. The bird confirmed it."

The bird, a common house finch, declined to comment but did tilt its head in a way that a nearby film student described as "incredibly cinematic."

Upon achieving protagonist status, Kevin immediately attempted to start his hero's journey by walking purposefully toward the horizon. He got approximately six blocks before forgetting why he had left the apartment and returning home to scroll his phone for ninety minutes. Sources close to Kevin say this is consistent with his general approach to life objectives.

"The hero's journey has a lot of steps," Kevin explained while reheating pasta he found in the back of the fridge that was at minimum four days old. "I'm on the refusal of the call stage. That can last years."

Experts at the Institute for Narrative Determinism, a think tank Kevin founded in his kitchen approximately twenty minutes after the bird incident, confirmed that refusal of the call is indeed a valid stage but noted that Kevin had already refused seven previous calls including a job offer, a jury duty summons, and a wedding invitation from his own brother.

"At a certain point, refusing the call just becomes procrastination with literary framing," said Dr. Angela Moore, who is not affiliated with Kevin's institute but was willing to be quoted because she found the whole situation deeply entertaining. "The main character energy is real, but so is the main character unemployment check."

Kevin has since begun documenting his protagonist journey on a vision board that currently contains a picture of a mountain, the word FORTUNE in magazine cutout letters, and a coupon for trail mix. He has not yet purchased a ukulele. He has however purchased the book Atomic Habits by James Clear, which he describes as "essentially the instruction manual for being the main character of your own life," before placing it on his nightstand where it has remained untouched for three weeks.

"The book is doing its work just by being there," Kevin insisted. "Proximity is a habit."
