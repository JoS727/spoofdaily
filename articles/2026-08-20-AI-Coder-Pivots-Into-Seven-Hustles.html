Local Man Learns Coding From AI, Immediately Pivots Into Seven Unrelated Hustles

By The Editorial Desk · Spoof Daily · August 20, 2026

SAN DIEGO, CA. Local fractional CTO and certified "idea guy" Marcus Delgado, 34, announced this week that he has learned to code after a 45-minute session with an AI assistant. Within the hour, he had also launched a dropshipping store, minted an NFT collection, started a podcast, registered six LLCs, and pivoted his entire identity no fewer than three times.

"The AI wrote the code, so I figured why stop there," Delgado told reporters from his living room, which is now also a co-working space, a content studio, and a 401(k) consulting firm. "It told me I could build anything. So I built everything. I have a SaaS tool for dog groomers, a crypto wallet for cats, and a subscription box that delivers one single olive to your door every month. It is called Olive You. The AI came up with the name. I came up with the price."

Delgado's AI assistant, which he has named "Co-Founder" and listed on his LinkedIn as an executive partner, reportedly suggested that he "lean into synergies" and "disrupt the disruption paradigm." Delgado nodded along despite having no idea what either phrase meant. He has since printed both sentences on business cards, which he hands out at networking events he hosts in his own kitchen.

"Before the AI, I was just a guy with a laptop and too many browser tabs," Delgado explained while opening his forty-seventh browser tab. "Now I am a multi-hyphenate serial entrepreneur. The AI said I should call myself a 'full-stack human.' I do not know what that means, but I put it on my email signature immediately."

Friends and family expressed concern about Delgado's rapid expansion into markets he has no experience in, no interest in, and in several cases, no actual customers for. His mother, Linda, pointed out that he previously spent two weeks learning woodworking from the same AI before abandoning it to start a meal prep delivery service that consists entirely of sandwiches he buys from a gas station and resells at a 400% markup.

"He called it 'artisanal proximity fuel,'" Linda said, shaking her head. "It is a gas station sandwich, Marcus."

Delgado's most recent venture, announced approximately eleven minutes before this article was written, is a consulting service that teaches other people how to use AI to start businesses. He charges $497 for a course titled "Zero to Seventeen Businesses in Sixty Minutes." The course consists of a single PDF that says "just ask the AI" in size 72 font.

When asked whether he plans to actually finish any of his projects, Delgado stared blankly, opened another browser tab, and whispered, "What if I started a farm."

At press time, Delgado had registered his eighth LLC, this one for a service that rents out his own brain as a "neural co-processor." He has zero customers but assures us the AI said it was "a really strong vertical."
