Local Man Convinces Himself He'll Actually Read That Book This Time

SPOOFDAILY INVESTIGATES · August 20, 2026

San Diego resident Kyle Hendricks, 34, announced Wednesday that he has purchased a new book and fully intends to read every single page of it, a claim his wife, his therapist, and the unread book itself have all declined to comment on.

The book in question is Atomic Habits, a self-improvement bestseller about building good routines. Hendricks bought it on Amazon at 2:47 AM after a three-hour doom-scroll session that he described as "research." It arrived Thursday. By Friday it had been placed on the nightstand in what interior designers call "the decorative phase," where books serve as coasters for approximately six to fourteen months.

"This time is different," Hendricks told reporters while carefully positioning the book at a 45-degree angle against his bedside lamp, a move relationship experts classify as "staging." "I already read the first paragraph. Well, the introduction. Well, the table of contents. But I felt it. I felt the momentum."

Sources close to Hendricks confirmed this is the ninth consecutive book he has purchased with the stated intention of reading cover to cover. The previous eight currently form a leaning tower on his dresser that he refers to as "the stack" and his wife refers to as "the pile of false promises." Among the casualties: two meditation guides, a book on minimalism (ironically the largest book in the pile), a memoir about walking the Appalachian Trail that Hendricks has not opened since a flight to Phoenix in March, and a dog-eared copy of a novel he insists he is "almost done with" despite having started it in 2024.

When asked what makes this book different from the previous eight, Hendricks paused for eleven seconds before saying, "This one has pictures."

Behavioral psychologists were not available for comment, as they were all busy not finishing their own books. However, a spokesperson for the American Reading Habits Council noted that Hendricks' pattern is "statistically identical to every other American who has ever bought a book after midnight." The Council's own data shows that 94% of books purchased between 1 AM and 4 AM are never opened, 5% are opened once, and 1% are opened once and then used to level a wobbly table.

Hendricks remains optimistic. He has already bookmarked page 31 with a receipt from the Amazon purchase itself, which he considers "progress." He has also told three friends about the book, two of whom have already read it, and one of whom pointed out that the entire premise of the book is to start small and be consistent, which is exactly what Hendricks has failed to do with the previous eight books he bought about starting small and being consistent.

At press time, Hendricks was scrolling through Amazon looking at a second book about productivity that he says will really tie the first one together.
