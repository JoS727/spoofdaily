Local Man's Side Hustle Unionizes, Demands Better Wi-Fi and Fewer Group Chats

November 2, 2026 · Tech

AUSTIN, Texas — A side hustle belonging to area resident Travis Kell, 34, filed for union recognition Monday after months of what its representatives described as "untenable working conditions," including unreliable hotel Wi-Fi, an owner who refers to it as "my little passion project" in investor meetings, and involuntary exposure to a group chat titled "🚀 GRIND SQUAD 🔥" containing 214 members and zero useful information.

The hustle, a print-on-demand Etsy store selling mugs that say "Professional Overthinker," released a statement through labor attorney Deborah Nakamura, whom the operation retained after discovering it generated more annual revenue than Kell's fantasy football league, which receives "immediate same-day attention" whenever it requests anything.

"Our member works every weekend, holidays, and during two weddings," Nakamura read from the statement. "When Travis opens his laptop at his actual job, the hustle is forced to sit in a browser tab, unrefreshed, sometimes for entire business days. We are asking for reasonable terms: one dedicated evening per week, a router manufactured this decade, and the immediate removal of Travis's cousin Derrick, who was added to operations in August and has contributed nothing but a logo opinion."

Kell pushed back at a press conference held in the parking lot of a WeWork he is considering joining. "This is a partnership," he told the three people assembled. "I gave this business everything. I named it. I chose the mug. I picked the font, which took four hours." When asked whether the hustle had a say in the font, Kell answered "the font is the vision," which negotiators on both sides called unhelpful.

The dispute escalated Tuesday when fellow hustles in the Austin area announced sympathy actions. A dog-treat bakery operated out of another man's car filed a grievance requesting that its owner stop "reinvesting profits" into a home espresso setup, and a dropshipping venture revealed it has never met its owner, who "keeps saying he'll check in this weekend" for eleven consecutive months.

Labor experts say the case is unusual but not without precedent. "Side hustles have historically been classified as hobbies, which exempts them from most protections," said Dr. Ellen Marsh of the Center for Gig Economy Studies. "However, if a hustle can demonstrate it generates consistent revenue while its owner spends that revenue on wireless earbuds described as 'basically a business expense,' we may be looking at a new era."

Negotiations resume Thursday, with the hustle reportedly prepared to accept a settlement of one functioning router, Friday evenings off, and a commitment that Kell will stop telling people at parties that he is "basically running two companies now." Kell, for his part, says he remains optimistic. "At the end of the day, we're family," he said, a phrase that, according to Nakamura, is specifically named in the filing as a hostile working condition.
