Tech CEO Discovers Entire Engineering Team Is One Guy Using Five Slack Accounts

CloudScale AI raised $42 million on the strength of its 40-person engineering team. Yesterday, CEO Diane Walsh discovered that team is one developer named Brian who has been running five Slack accounts and a spreadsheet of fictional personalities since the seed round.

SAN FRANCISCO For 14 months, CloudScale AI was the darling of the Bay Area tech scene. The pitch deck described a "world-class engineering team of 40 distributed across six time zones," and investors ate it up. The Series B closed at $42 million with a $310 million valuation. There was just one problem: the entire engineering department was one man named Brian Kempke, 29, sitting in a Sacramento apartment with three monitors and a Dry-Erase board mapping out who was "on vacation" this week.

"I started to get suspicious during the quarterly all-hands," Walsh told reporters. "Our lead backend engineer, 'Sven,' delivered a presentation from a webcam that appeared to be in the same room as our DevOps lead, 'Priya.' Same lamp. Same dying houseplant. I asked Brian about it and he said they were 'co-working buddies.' I didn't push because the shipping velocity was incredible."

Brian was indeed shipping. CloudScale's entire product was built by one person. "Sven" handled backend. "Priya" did infrastructure. "Marcus" was frontend. "Dave" was the new hire "still onboarding" and therefore never had to speak. Brian voiced all five during standups, muting and unmuting with what colleagues called "suspiciously seamless" precision.

The Discovery

The scheme unraveled last Tuesday when Walsh messaged Sven, Priya, and Marcus simultaneously. All three replied within four seconds, all using the word "indeed" in the same grammatically unusual position. Walsh called an emergency meeting and demanded cameras on. What followed was 11 minutes of what witnesses call "the worst one-man show in Silicon Valley history." Brian toggled between five Zoom windows with different virtual backgrounds and a hastily drawn mustache on one account. He eventually held up a note reading "I CAN EXPLAIN."

"The investors wanted a team. I gave them a team. The product works, the code is clean, and honestly 'Dave' was the best engineer we had. Dave never complained, never had PTO conflicts, and never argued about TypeScript. Dave was a visionary."

Brian's spreadsheet contained detailed profiles for each team member. Sven was from Stockholm and "allergic to small talk." Priya owned cats named Kernel and Semaphore. Marcus had strong opinions about semicolons. Brian had been arguing with himself in Slack for over a year, generating 38,000 messages of synthetic workplace banter.

The Aftermath

CloudScale's investors were furious, then confused. "We funded 40 engineers and got one guy," said lead investor Mark Halpert. "We overpaid by a factor of 40. But Brian built the entire product solo in 14 months. Should I fire him or promote him?"
