Local Artist Presses Vinyl Instead of Just Streaming Like Normal Humans

SPOOF DAILY | August 31, 2026 | Satire Desk

SAN DIEGO, CA. In a move that has baffled streaming platforms, confused record labels, and alarmed at least one roommate, San Diego dark pop artist Calitoy has announced plans to press the upcoming XCalitoy album to vinyl in September 2026. Yes, vinyl. That thing your dad keeps in a milk crate in the garage. That thing that requires a machine that spins around and a needle that costs more than your rent. That thing.

"I wanted the music to be physical," Calitoy told reporters, as if that were a sentence that made sense in the year 2026. "I wanted people to hold it."

We checked. You can already hold your phone. The music is already on it. The phone is in your hand. The music is in the phone that is in your hand. You are holding the music right now. But apparently that was not enough for Calitoy, who needed the music to also exist as a flat disc that requires a dedicated machine, a clean needle, a dust-free environment, and the patience to flip it over halfway through.

Eighty-Six Tracks and Counting

Before you dismiss this as the behavior of a reasonable person, consider that Calitoy has uploaded eighty-six tracks to SoundCloud. Eighty-six. Most artists release ten songs and call it a career. Calitoy released eighty-six and then decided the thing that was missing was a format that was popular when your grandmother was dating.

"Each track was a study," Calitoy said, unconvincingly. We checked with several musicologists, who confirmed that most people call those "songs" and move on with their lives.

The Kurced Debut Album

The vinyl in question will contain the Kurced debut album, a ten-track project that Calitoy describes as "an era" rather than an album, which is the kind of thing you say when you have been making music alone in a room for too long and have started inventing new vocabulary for normal things.

Buy a Turntable, We Guess

If you are going to insist on owning music in a format from the previous century, you will need equipment. This is your life now.

Find a Turntable

We spoke to a vinyl pressing plant employee named Doug, who asked that we not use his last name because his boss does not know he spent forty-five minutes talking to a satire website. "Oh yeah, we get a few of these a month," Doug said. "Artist wants to press vinyl. We press vinyl. It is a living. Most of them end up in a box in someone's apartment next to a turntable they bought at a flea market and never set up. But the music is good, I'll say that. The music is good."

When asked whether Calitoy was aware that streaming existed, that Spotify had not gone anywhere, that Bandcamp was right there, and that the entire music industry had collectively agreed that physical media was a nice idea for people who had room in their apartments, Calitoy reportedly said, "Yes," and then went back to working on a 3D world for the music to live in, because apparently songs now need real estate.

The Roommate Weighs In

"I came home and there were test pressings on the kitchen table," said the roommate, who asked to remain anonymous because they did not want to be associated with a vinyl pressing in any capacity. "I asked what they were. Calitoy said 'the album.' I said 'the album is on SoundCloud, I have listened to it, it has eighty-six tracks, I have heard it.' Calitoy said 'this is the physical version.' I said 'I physically have a phone.' We stared at each other for a while. It was a long evening."

At press time, Calitoy was seen carefully placing a test pressing on a turntable, lowering the needle with the focus of a surgeon, and then sitting in a folding chair in the middle of the living room listening to side A with eyes closed. The roommate was in the other room streaming the same album on a phone. Both reported enjoying the experience. Only one of them had spent four hundred dollars on equipment to do it.
