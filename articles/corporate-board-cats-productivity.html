Corporate Board Replaces All Executives With Cats, Productivity Soars 300%

OmniCorp Industries fired its entire C-suite and replaced them with six shelter cats. Within two weeks, meetings dropped to zero, email volume plummeted, and the stock price doubled. Analysts are "deeply confused."

CHICAGO — In what is being called the most successful corporate restructuring since someone invented the phrase "synergistic alignment," OmniCorp Industries announced last Tuesday that it has replaced its entire executive leadership team with six cats adopted from the Cook County Animal Shelter. The results, according to every measurable metric, have been extraordinary.

"We were skeptical at first," said OmniCorp shareholder Reggie Martinez. "Then the new CEO — a tabby named Mr. Boots — knocked the quarterly losses report off the table, and honestly, that was the most decisive leadership action this company has seen in a decade."

The Numbers Don't Lie

In the 14 days since the feline leadership transition, OmniCorp has reported a 300% increase in overall productivity, a 100% reduction in meetings (from 847 per week to zero), and a 94% drop in internal email volume. The remaining 6% of emails are all calendar invites that were accidentally sent by a cat walking across a keyboard, which employees describe as "still more useful than the old executive memos."

"The previous CEO spent forty minutes in a meeting explaining his vision for 'agile innovation ecosystems.' Mr. Boots sat on the keyboard for six seconds and accidentally created a Slack channel that actually improved cross-team communication. The ROI on the keyboard incident alone was $2.3 million."

— OmniCorp internal report, written by an employee who has never been happier at work

Each Cat, A Department

The six-cat executive team has been structured with the kind of organizational clarity that eluded OmniCorp's human leadership for years:

  • Mr. Boots (CEO): Spends 80% of the workday napping on the quarterly report. The other 20% is spent knocking pens off the desk, which the board has interpreted as "bold risk-taking."
  • Pumpkin (CFO): A calico who sits on financial documents and refuses to move. Expenses have dropped dramatically because no one can access the budget.
  • Shadow (CTO): A black cat who only appears when the Wi-Fi goes down, which the IT department has started calling "divine intervention."
  • Whiskers (CHRO): An orange tabby whose primary HR initiative is "everyone gets to leave at 5 PM because I want the office to myself." Employee satisfaction is at an all-time high.
  • Snowball (CMO): A white cat who stares at the marketing team until they change the campaign. They always change the campaign. The new campaigns are better.
  • Gerald (COO): A tuxedo cat who operates the coffee machine by sitting on it. Operations have never run more smoothly.

What Happens Next

Wall Street's response has been, in a word, bewildering. OmniCorp's stock surged 210% in the two weeks following the transition, with analysts at Goldman Sachs issuing a report titled "We Don't Understand This But We're Not Going to Fight It." Morgan Stanley upgraded the stock to "Buy" with a note that simply read: "Cats. Strong buy."

The former human executives, reached for comment, had mixed reactions. Former CEO Richard Pemberton, who earned $43 million annually and held 847 meetings per week, said he was "actually relieved" and has started a Substack about the experience. Former CFO Linda Vance has begun fostering shelter cats in what she describes as "a journey of personal growth and also possibly revenge."

As for Mr. Boots, when asked about the company's five-year strategic plan, he yawned, stretched, and walked away — a response that shareholders unanimously praised as "the most honest thing a CEO has ever said about a five-year plan."
