Anthropic IPO Filing Includes $200B Revenue Forecast, Complimentary Crystal Ball

The company, which has barely turned a profit, projects $200 billion in 2028 revenue. The filing also includes a prayer candle, a vision board, and a Post-it note that says "trust us bro."

SD
Spoof Daily Staff
Published Aug 18, 2026 · 6 min read · 100% Made Up

SAN FRANCISCO — As Anthropic prepares for what could be the largest AI IPO in history, the company's S-1 filing has revealed a revenue forecast so ambitious that it includes a complimentary crystal ball, a prayer candle labeled "FY2028," and a handwritten note from CEO Dario Amodei that reads: "Trust us, bro."

The filing, submitted to the SEC last week, projects that Anthropic will generate $190 to $200 billion in revenue by 2028. This is a bold claim for a company that reported $11.5 billion in Q2 revenue and has, according to its own admission, "barely turned a profit." When asked about the gap between current revenue and the 2028 forecast, an Anthropic spokesperson said the company was "very confident in the power of vibes-based financial modeling."

The Forecast: How They Got There

The S-1 filing, which is 847 pages long and includes 312 pages of risk factors alone, reveals the methodology behind the $200 billion forecast. According to page 714, the projection is based on three scenarios:

  • Scenario A (The Optimistic Case): Every person on Earth uses Claude for everything. Every email is written by Claude. Every text message is written by Claude. Every grocery list, every love letter, every passive-aggressive Slack message to a coworker named Kyle. Revenue: $200 billion.
  • Scenario B (The Realistic Case): Only most people on Earth use Claude for most things. Revenue: $190 billion. This is the "conservative" estimate.
  • Scenario C (The Worst Case): The company admits this scenario exists but has written it in invisible ink. It is rumored to involve the words "we go bankrupt" and "Dario starts a podcast."
"Our revenue forecast is based on a proprietary methodology we call 'aspirational extrapolation.' We look at our current revenue. We look at where we want to be. We draw a line between the two. The line goes up. If the line does not go up, we redraw it until it does. This is settled finance."

The Goodies Bag

In what appears to be a first for SEC filings, Anthropic included a "goodies bag" with the S-1 document. The bag, which was hand-delivered to SEC headquarters in a recyclable tote, contained:

  • One (1) crystal ball: Etched with the Anthropic logo. A Post-it on the ball reads: "For revenue forecasting purposes only."
  • One (1) prayer candle: Labeled "FY2028." The candle is vanilla-scented, which the filing notes is "the scent of confidence."
  • One (1) vision board: A poster board covered in cut-out magazine photos of money, rockets, and a man who appears to be Jeff Bezos but might just be a guy with a similar bald pattern.
  • One (1) Post-it note: Handwritten by Amodei. It says: "Trust us, bro." The "bro" is underlined twice.
  • One (1) copy of "The Secret": The 2006 self-help book about the law of attraction. It is dog-eared on the chapter about "manifesting wealth."

The SEC has not commented on the goodies bag, though one anonymous regulator was overheard saying: "I've reviewed a lot of IPO filings in my career. This is the first one that came with a candle."

The Skeptics

Not everyone is convinced. "The $200 billion forecast assumes that Anthropic's revenue will grow at a rate that, if plotted on a graph, would look like a hockey stick designed by someone who has never seen a hockey stick," said financial analyst Patricia Vance. "They're projecting 17x growth in two years. For context, that's like a lemonade stand projecting it will become Coca-Cola by Thursday."

Even Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, in a candid moment during the IPO roadshow, admitted that AI suffers from a crisis of trust, noting that people worry companies are "cooking up some new way to screw them over." He then clarified that the $200 billion forecast was "definitely not us screwing anyone over" and that the crystal ball was "for decorative purposes only."

When pressed on whether a company that has barely turned a profit should be valued at $2 trillion, Amodei responded: "Have you seen our crystal ball? It's really nice. It's got our logo on it."
