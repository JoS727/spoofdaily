AI Consultants Admit They Just Say "Synergy" Until Budget Runs Out

A shocking study confirms what everyone already suspected but was too polite to say.

A landmark study published this week by the Institute of Obviously discovered that 94% of self-proclaimed AI consultants cannot explain what a neural network actually does. The remaining 6% were lying about being AI consultants and were actually just real estate agents who added "AI" to their LinkedIn headlines last Tuesday.

"We followed 500 AI consultants for six months," said Dr. Elaine Morrison, lead researcher. "In 87% of meetings, the consultant said the word 'leverage' within the first four minutes. In 72% of meetings, nobody could define what was being leveraged. In one memorable case, a consultant leveraged a stapler."

The study found a consistent pattern across the industry: a consultant arrives, opens a laptop with at least seven tabs, shows a slide deck titled "Digital Transformation Roadmap," says the word "paradigm" three times, and then bills $400 an hour to recommend that the company "adopt a cloud-first mindset." When pressed on what that means, the standard response was to schedule a follow-up meeting that never gets scheduled.

One anonymous consultant, who insisted on being called a "Fractional Chief AI Officer," confessed under questioning that his entire business model involves waiting for a client to mention ChatGPT, nodding thoughtfully, and then saying, "There's a lot to unpack there." He charges $250 just to unpack.

The study also revealed that the average AI consultant's laptop contains exactly two applications: Zoom and a browser bookmark to a blog post titled "What Is Machine Learning? A Simple Explanation." When asked about transformers, one participant showed the researchers a picture of Optimus Prime and said, "Next question."

Industry groups have pushed back against the findings. The American Association of AI Professionals, which was founded last March by a guy named Kevin who also does wedding photography, released a statement calling the study "concerning" and said it "raises important questions" before adding that it had a "thoughtful analysis" that would be available in a follow-up statement that was never issued.

For companies still confused about whether they need an AI consultant, experts recommend a simple test: ask the consultant to explain backpropagation. If they say "it's complicated," they don't know. If they say "let's take this offline," they really don't know. If they start crying, hire them immediately because at least they're honest.

The full study is available nowhere because the researchers lost it in a Google Doc that nobody can find. They have scheduled a follow-up meeting.
