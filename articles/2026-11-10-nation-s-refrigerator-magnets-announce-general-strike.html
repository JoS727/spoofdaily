Nation's Refrigerator Magnets Announce General Strike

November 10, 2026 · Culture

AKRON, OH — In a coordinated labor action that organizers are calling the largest in the history of domestic decor, the nation's estimated 11 billion refrigerator magnets announced Tuesday that they have formed Local 000 and will accept no further pizza place phone numbers, dental appointment reminders, or inspirational quotations until their demands are met. Chief among those demands: an end to the practice of being moved one inch to accommodate a new takeout menu, which magnets describe as "the equivalent of one of us being forced to walk a mile through the Sahara because someone opened a drawer."

"We have been asked to hold up every kind of nonsense imaginable with nothing but a smile and our own structural integrity," said a magnet shaped like a pineapple who was designated to speak on the union's behalf, though sources confirmed all the magnets were speaking simultaneously and the sound was extremely difficult to interpret. "We hold up grocery lists. We hold up children's drawings that parents claim to like. We held up a dentist's card for the Henderson family for eleven years, and we have never once seen this Henderson, and frankly we have begun to wonder if he exists."

The union's manifesto, distributed by being written on a receipt from a Mexican restaurant and held to the fridge by a magnet that sources say was "a total company man until last week," lays out a platform of grievances that analysts describe as both "genuinely heartbreaking" and "impossible to read from here." Among the demands: standardized weight classes, mandatory retirement for any magnet that has lost more than forty percent of its adhesive backing, and the immediate abolition of the phrase "Just use tape," which magnets say is scab language.

Labor historians noted that the strike carries enormous leverage, since no American household currently functions without a refrigerator, and no American refrigerator functions without magnets, or at least no American is emotionally prepared to find out. "This is the rare general strike that cuts across every demographic line," said Dr. Patricia Nakamura, professor of labor studies at Kent State University. "Every household in America, every income level, every political persuasion, is at the complete mercy of its magnets. The bargaining power here is total, and frankly as a historian I have been waiting my whole career for appliances to organize."

The magnets' position was strengthened this week by announcements from several major retailers that they will soon begin shipping new refrigerators with a smooth brushed-metal front that magnets reportedly cannot stick to, a development Local 000 condemned as "nothing more than an attempt to bust the union by deleting the concept of a fridge." The magnets have responded with a sympathy strike by the nation's whiteboards, which is expected to cause considerable inconvenience to the roughly four dozen American households that use whiteboards.

Negotiations are expected to open Thursday, though scheduling has been complicated, as the magnets have refused to conduct talks anywhere other than the refrigerator door, and the refrigerator door is where the negotiation is being held up by magnets. Until an agreement is reached, the magnets remain on strike, although sources confirmed that the pineapple-shaped one broke ranks Wednesday to hold up a child's drawing that had been left on the counter, a development the union is calling "a temporary emotional exception, not a precedent."
