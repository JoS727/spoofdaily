San Diego Music Scene: Everyone's Releasing Albums in September

SPOOF DAILY | August 31, 2026 | Satire Desk

SAN DIEGO, CA. We at Spoof Daily have conducted a thorough investigation into the San Diego music scene and have uncovered a disturbing pattern: every single musician in the city has apparently agreed to release their album in September 2026. We do not know why. We do not know how. We suspect a group chat. But the evidence is overwhelming and the release calendar is suspicious.

We contacted the San Diego Music Commission to ask if this was coordinated. They did not respond, which we interpret as either "no comment" or "we also do not know what is happening." Both possibilities are equally concerning.

The Suspects

Calitoy / Kurced

Dark Pop · 86 SoundCloud tracks · XCalitoy vinyl

First on our list is Calitoy, who has been quietly uploading tracks to SoundCloud like a person filling a journal nobody asked for, and has now accumulated eighty-six of them. For context, most albums have ten songs. Calitoy has eight albums worth of material on SoundCloud and is only now releasing a debut. The debut is called Kurced, because apparently naming an album was too straightforward and it needed to be rebranded as an "era."

The ten tracks are: "Locked Out," "Todos Americanos," "Lioness Lost Hard Tides," "Lioness Lost Unfinished Tides," "Self," "Be Alone," "Lantern in the Fog," "For," "Always There," and "Not You." We notice that "Lioness Lost" appears twice with different subtitles, which is either a creative choice or a filing error. We choose to believe it is a filing error because it is funnier that way.

Calitoy is also pressing the album to vinyl via XCalitoy, because streaming was apparently too convenient for everyone involved.

Mara Velez

Indie Folk · Singer-Songwriter · September album

Mara Velez makes indie folk, which is the genre equivalent of ordering tea at a bar. It is good tea. It is probably the best tea in the city. But it is tea, and the bar is full of people doing shots, and Mara is in the corner with a guitar and a whole album of songs about feelings that are going to make you sit with your emotions in a way you did not consent to. September. Of course.

Sol Reyes

Ambient · Electronic · September album

Sol Reyes makes ambient electronic music, which means she recorded some wind and put a synthesizer under it and is releasing it as an album in September. We are not saying it is not good. We are saying it is wind with a synth under it and it is coming out in the same month as eight other albums. Someone should have suggested a different month. Nobody suggested a different month.

The Coral Frequency

Dream Pop · Shoegaze · September album

The Coral Frequency is a duo whose name sounds like a sunscreen brand and whose music sounds like what happens when you fall asleep with headphones on. They are releasing a full-length in September, because of course they are. We are starting to think September is the only month that exists in San Diego. The other eleven months are just preparation for September.

June Okafor

Jazz · Soul · September album

June Okafor makes jazz, which means she is the only person on this list who can actually read music, and she is releasing her album in September alongside a dark pop artist, a folk singer, a wind recorder, and a sunscreen brand. This is what coordination looks like when nobody coordinated.

But Why September

We asked several of the artists why September. Calitoy said something about "the work being ready," which is the kind of thing artists say when they do not have a marketing plan. Mara Velez said "it felt right," which is the kind of answer that makes journalists want to become accountants. Sol Reyes said something about the autumnal equinox, which we are not verifying because it sounds made up and also we do not care.

The most convincing answer came from a bartender at a venue in North Park who asked not to be named because he does not want to be quoted in a satire article. "They all just made albums," he said. "That is what musicians do. They make albums. It is September because the albums are done. You want them to hold them until February? They are artists, not a supply chain."

He has a point, and we resent him for it.

The Real Problem

The real problem is not that everyone is releasing in September. The real problem is that they are all good. Calitoy has eighty-six tracks of proof that the dark pop is real. Kurced is a debut that actually sounds like a debut should, which is to say like the culmination of something rather than the beginning of a content strategy. The other artists are also, irritatingly, talented.

This means that in September 2026, you will need to listen to approximately five new San Diego albums, and you will not be able to dismiss any of them as filler. This is an inconvenience, and we hold the San Diego music scene personally responsible.

At press time, a sixth artist had announced a September release date. We are choosing not to name them because we are tired and also because this article is already long enough.
