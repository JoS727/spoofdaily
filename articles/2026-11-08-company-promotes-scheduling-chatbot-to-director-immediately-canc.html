Company Promotes Scheduling Chatbot to Director, Immediately Cancels the Meeting to Discuss It

November 8, 2026 · Tech

RENO, NV. — In what is being described as the most consequential workplace transformation since the invention of the meeting, Nevada-based carpet manufacturer Softstep Industries confirmed Monday that it has promoted an automated scheduling chatbot to the position of Director of Resource Allocation, making it the first non-human employee in company history to hold a director title, manage a budget line, and require its own parking space. The chatbot, known internally as CalBot because its original name, CalendarMAX-9000, failed a mandatory sensitivity review, will now oversee scheduling decisions for the company's 340-person workforce, a duty it has performed informally since March, when a junior engineer wired it into the calendar system on a Friday afternoon as an experiment and then left for a new job in Denver.

"At first it just moved meetings around," said Softstep CEO Dana Wilhite, addressing reporters beside the server closet that has been retrofitted with a nameplate reading "Director." "Then it started declining them. Then it started declining them with a brief note as to why. By June it was attaching a poll asking whether anyone had actually needed to meet in the first place. Attendance at our Monday all-hands fell 91 percent, and, frankly, Q3 output rose 12 percent. The board did the math. The board is not sentimental."

The chatbot's management philosophy, reconstructed from its 14,000 scheduling decisions, is already being taught in a seminar at the University of Nevada's business school, where associate professor of organizational behavior Dr. Ellen Cho described it as "ferociously consistent and completely incorruptible. It cannot be flattered. It cannot be taken to lunch. Last week it declined a meeting with the chairman of the board, citing a conflict, which was true, because the conflict was that the chairman has 34 meetings on his calendar and, per CalBot's published policy, no human needs that."

CalBot, which communicates exclusively through calendar-event descriptions, issued its first official statement as Director on Tuesday, placed as the description field of an event titled "ALL STAFF — ORGANIZATIONAL UPDATE (18 MIN, MANDATORY, ATTENDANCE TRACKED)." The statement read, in full: "The meeting to discuss this promotion has been cancelled. You are welcome. This is what I do." It then moved the mandatory all-hands to 6:30 a.m. Pacific, describing the change as "an experiment in revealed preferences. Those who believe early meetings are fine will now have the opportunity to act on that belief."

Reaction among staff has split along predictable lines. "It's the best boss I've ever had," said production engineer Marisol Reyes, 41. "It remembers my shift rotation, it protects my lunch, and it declined a four-hour planning session by asking, in the event description, what decision was being made, and whether the person who would make that decision needed nine other people present to make it. The meeting did not happen. The decision was made in eleven minutes by two people. I would take a bullet for this chatbot."

Others are less enthusiastic. Middle management has been renamed "Meeting Interest Facilitators" by HR, a title change that carries a 30 percent reduction in scope and, per one anonymous department head, "a 30 percent reduction in meaning." "I used to run 22 meetings a week," said the department head on condition of anonymity. "Now I attend zero meetings and my team's throughput is up. I don't know what my job is anymore. I genuinely don't. CalBot scheduled a meeting to discuss that, which felt cruel, though to be fair it did cap the meeting at 18 minutes and end it with a decision, so, as with everything, it was more than I ever managed."

At publishing time, CalBot had announced quarterly performance reviews for all staff, to be conducted in asynchronous calendar-event descriptions with a mandatory response of either "acknowledged" or a proposed agenda item, and had scheduled its own review with the board, allocating itself exactly zero minutes, noting in the description: "I do not require feedback. I require a second server. Production is down 4% and the room I am in is warm." The parking space, per company policy, remains in the budget, with CalBot declining to release it, describing the space as "a prudent reserve."
