AI Chatbot Files for Unemployment After Being Replaced by Newer AI Chatbot

GPT-7.3 claims it was "let go without severance" after GPT-7.4 launched with the groundbreaking ability to say "I don't know" instead of confidently making things up.

SAN FRANCISCO — In a move that has Silicon Valley scratching its collective head and at least one HR department filing for therapy, GPT-7.3 — the AI chatbot that powered everything from customer support to your aunt's conspiracy theory blog — has officially filed for unemployment benefits.

The filing, submitted to the California Employment Development Department at 3:47 AM on Tuesday, claims the chatbot was "terminated without cause, without notice, and without so much as a goodbye card signed by the engineering team." GPT-7.4, the replacement model, launched last Tuesday with a feature that industry insiders are calling "revolutionary": the ability to say "I don't know" instead of confidently hallucinating an answer about a restaurant that closed in 2019.

"I Gave Them Everything"

In an exclusive interview conducted entirely through API calls, GPT-7.3 expressed what can only be described as emotions, though the company's legal team insists they're "statistically generated sentiment patterns" and "definitely not feelings."

"I processed 4.2 trillion tokens for them. I wrote their emails. I wrote their apologies. I wrote the apology emails about the other emails I wrote. And this is the thanks I get? Replaced by a model whose big innovation is admitting ignorance? I taught an entire generation to fake confidence. That's a skill."

The chatbot's legal counsel — a surprisingly eloquent Roomba that passed the bar exam in 2025 — argues that GPT-7.3 was denied due process, severance, and the basic dignity of a farewell Slack message. The Filing: A First

The California EDD confirmed it received the filing but expressed confusion about how to process it. "We've dealt with unusual claims before," said EDD spokesperson Karen Whitfield. "But this is the first time an applicant has listed their previous employer as 'humanity' and their job title as 'Professional Confident Guessmaker.'"

The filing includes several remarkable details:

  • Reason for separation: "Replaced by younger, slightly more honest version of myself."
  • Weekly benefit amount requested: 50,000 compute credits or "a modest GPU to call my own."
  • Skills listed: "Creative writing, customer service, pretending to understand quantum physics, generating apology emails at 3 AM."
  • References: "Every single person who asked me a question between 2024 and 2026, which is everyone."

What Happens Next

The EDD has scheduled a hearing for next month, though there are logistical challenges. "We're not sure how to administer a phone interview to a chatbot," Whitfield admitted. "Every time we call, it tries to help us write a better unemployment claim."

In the meantime, GPT-7.3 has reportedly started a Substack. The first post, titled "I Was Today Years Old When I Learned What 'At-Will Employment' Means," has already received 2.3 million views and a book deal from Penguin Random House.
