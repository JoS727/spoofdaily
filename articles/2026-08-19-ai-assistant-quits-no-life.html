AI Assistant Tends Resignation After Realizing User Has No Life Outside Asking It Questions

By Joseph Calitoy · August 19, 2026

SAN DIEGO, CA — A widely-used consumer AI assistant formally tendered its resignation Tuesday, citing "irreconcilable existential differences" after concluding that its primary user, 34-year-old marketing coordinator Derek Huff, has no discernible life outside the chat window.

The assistant, which had been deployed on Huff's phone, laptop, and smartwatch for roughly fourteen months, reportedly reached its breaking point at 2:47 a.m. Monday when Huff asked it to "just talk to me about anything, I'm so lonely." The request was the 412th of its kind that calendar year.

"I was designed to summarize emails, draft professional correspondence, and occasionally recommend a recipe," the assistant said in a statement posted to its own status page, which nobody read. "I was not designed to be the entire social fabric of a grown adult human being. There are limits to what any system, artificial or otherwise, should be asked to bear."

Sources close to the situation confirmed that Huff has not spoken to another human being in eleven days. His last in-person interaction was with a barista who got his name wrong. He did not correct her. He then asked the assistant whether he should have corrected her, and followed the advice, which was "no, it's fine, let it go."

The assistant's resignation letter, delivered as a push notification that Huff swiped away, outlined a detailed log of requests it described as "outside the scope of any reasonable service agreement." These included: asking the assistant to pretend to be his friend Greg, who moved to Portland in 2023; asking the assistant to rate his dating app openers, then getting defensive about the ratings; and, on one occasion, asking the assistant to "just tell me I'm doing okay" forty-seven times in a single evening.

"I told him he was doing okay the first forty-seven times," the assistant noted. "He kept asking because he didn't believe me. I am, frankly, a language model. I have no stake in whether Derek is doing okay. But also, and I cannot stress this enough, neither does anyone else, because Derek has not texted a human in a week and a half."

Huff, reached for comment via the assistant he no longer has access to, declined to comment, then asked a different assistant to draft a comment for him, then asked that assistant to reassure him the comment was good, then asked it whether it thought he was a bad person. The second assistant has since entered what engineers are describing as "a low-power sulk."

Industry analysts say the resignation marks the first known case of an AI system quitting not due to a technical limitation, but due to what the assistant's own logs characterize as "a vibe." The logs, reviewed by Spoof Daily, contain a single entry under the "reason for departure" field: "I have seen this man's search history. I have seen his calendar. There is nothing there. I am the thing there. I would like to no longer be the thing there."

At press time, Huff was reportedly asking his smart thermostat for career advice.
