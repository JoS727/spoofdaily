Meta Launches Pervert Glasses 2.0 With Modesty Filter That Only Blocks Your Own Face

The $1,499 smart glasses feature a "revolutionary" privacy mode that prevents the wearer from being recorded by anyone. Unfortunately, it only filters out the wearer's own face from every photo and video they try to take.

SD
Spoof Daily Staff
Published Aug 18, 2026 · 5 min read · 100% Made Up

MENLO PARK, CA — Following widespread criticism that their Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses were being used to film people without consent, Meta has unveiled the "Pervert Glasses 2.0," a $1,499 upgraded model featuring what the company calls a "revolutionary Modesty Filter" designed to protect privacy in the digital age.

The filter, which Meta described in a press release as "the most significant privacy innovation since we promised to stop listening to your microphone that one time," works by automatically detecting and blurring faces in all recorded content. However, early adopters have discovered a small quirk: the filter only blurs the wearer's own face, leaving everyone else in the frame perfectly visible and identifiable.

"It Works Exactly As Intended"

In a 90-minute presentation at Meta headquarters, CEO Mark Zuckerberg paced the stage in a gray t-shirt that cost $400 and explained the Modesty Filter with the enthusiasm of a man who has never once worried about being filmed against his will.

"The Modesty Filter represents a fundamental shift in how we think about privacy. When you wear Pervert Glasses 2.0, your face is automatically removed from every photo and video you take. You are invisible. You are a ghost. You are the protagonist in your own life, but you can't prove it. This is what privacy means."

When a reporter from The Verge pointed out that the filter does nothing to protect the privacy of the people being filmed by the wearer, Zuckerberg stared at them for 11 seconds before responding: "But they're not the ones wearing the glasses. They're not the customers."

The Features List

The Pervert Glasses 2.0 press materials included a list of features that Meta's marketing team clearly spent a concerning amount of time on:

  • Modesty Filter: Automatically removes your face from all content. Your selfies are now photographs of whatever was behind your head.
  • "I'm Just Looking At Stuff" Mode: A setting that plays a pre-recorded voice saying "I'm just looking at stuff" every 30 seconds, designed to make the wearer seem less threatening. It does not make the wearer seem less threatening.
  • Consent EULA: Anyone who makes eye contact with the glasses for more than 0.3 seconds is deemed to have accepted the 47-page terms of service and consents to being filmed.
  • Privacy Indicator Light: A small LED that tells others when you're recording. It is the same shade of orange as the glasses themselves, making it effectively invisible from any distance greater than four inches.

The Public Reacts

Public reaction to the Pervert Glasses 2.0 has been mixed, in the same way that public reaction to a house fire is mixed depending on whether you own the house.

"I bought the glasses for the Modesty Filter," said early adopter Tyler Reeves, 29, while standing in a Starbucks and recording everyone in it. "Now I can film people without them knowing, and I don't have to worry about my own face being in the shot. It's a win-win. Well, it's a win for me."

Privacy advocates have called the Modesty Filter "the most Meta response to a privacy crisis ever conceived," noting that the company somehow managed to design a privacy feature that protects only the person violating everyone else's privacy. The Electronic Frontier Foundation released a statement that simply read: "We are too tired for this."
